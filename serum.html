Development of Multiplex Assay using MSD platform

During my internship at the Serum Institute of India, I worked in the Clinical Bioanalytics Laboratory on a project focused on evaluating the effectiveness of pneumococcal vaccines using advanced immunoassay techniques. My project aimed to develop a multiplexed assay that could detect antibodies against multiple pneumococcal serotypes in a single experiment, improving efficiency compared to conventional methods that test each serotype individually. A key part of the project involved biotinylation of purified pneumococcal polysaccharides, where biotin molecules were attached to enable strong binding to streptavidin-coated assay plates. This interaction ensured stable immobilisation of the polysaccharides, allowing reliable antibody detection. Careful optimisation was required to ensure sufficient biotin attachment without altering the natural structure of the polysaccharides, as this could affect antibody recognition. The assay was performed using the Meso Scale Discovery (MSD) platform, which detects antibody binding through electrochemiluminescence. This method produces a light signal proportional to the amount of antibody present and allows multiple serotypes to be analysed simultaneously within a single well. Compared to traditional methods, this approach reduces sample volume, shortens assay time, and improves overall efficiency while maintaining high sensitivity. Throughout the project, I was involved in polysaccharide preparation, biotinylation, purification, plate immobilisation, and optimisation of assay conditions such as reagent concentrations and incubation times. These steps were essential to minimise background noise and ensure consistent and reliable results. This project provided valuable exposure to immunoassay development and highlighted the importance of analytical techniques in vaccine research and quality evaluation.
