Plant Tissue Culture

During my summer internship, I worked on a plant tissue culture project, which involves growing plant parts such as cells, tissues, or small sections of stems in a controlled laboratory environment rather than in soil. The main objective of this technique is to produce healthy, disease-free plants and to study how plants grow under carefully regulated conditions of light, temperature, and nutrients. A key part of the project was preparing nutrient-rich culture media containing essential macronutrients, micronutrients, vitamins, and plant growth regulators such as auxins and cytokinins, which control whether the plant develops roots or shoots. I gained hands-on experience in media preparation, sterilization using an autoclave to prevent contamination, and dispensing the media into sterile containers. I also performed microbial contamination checks by exposing culture plates to different lab environments and observing bacterial and fungal growth. Additionally, I worked with teak and bamboo plant cultures, where I excised plant parts and transferred them into fresh media under sterile conditions using a laminar airflow chamber. I monitored root development, identified contamination in cultures, and carried out subculturing to promote growth. In the later stages, I participated in the hardening process, where lab-grown plantlets were gradually adapted to natural conditions using coco peat and soil mixtures before being transferred to outdoor environments. This project provided practical insight into plant biotechnology techniques and highlighted the importance of sterility, nutrient balance, and environmental control in successful plant propagation.
