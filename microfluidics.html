Isolation of Exosome Released From Contractile Skeletal Muscle Using A Microfluidic Device

My capstone project comprised of two integrated components that combined the principles of biology and engineering to study and isolate nanoscale biological particles known as exosomes. In the first part, I designed and fabricated a microfluidic device using polydimethylsiloxane (PDMS), a flexible and biocompatible material commonly used for lab-on-chip systems. The device contained microscale channels that allowed controlled flow of cell culture fluid containing exosomes. Since exosomes possess a net negative surface charge, an external electric field was applied across the microchannel to induce electrophoretic movement, enabling their separation from other cellular components based on charge and size differences. This approach offers a more efficient and scalable alternative to conventional techniques such as ultracentrifugation. The biological samples used were derived from cultured skeletal muscle cells, where conditioned media was collected, pre-processed to remove debris, and then introduced into the device for separation. In the second part of the project, I focused on isolating skeletal muscle tissue and quantifying its contractile force. The isolated muscle was carefully mounted onto a mechanical setup using fine sutures and connected to a cantilever beam system. Upon stimulation, the muscle contraction generated a pulling force that caused measurable deflection of the cantilever. By analyzing this deflection using basic principles of beam mechanics, the force produced by the muscle was calculated in the milli-Newton range. This experiment provided insights into the mechanical properties of muscle tissue and required precise measurement techniques using microscopy and imaging tools. Together, these two components of the project demonstrated how microscale engineering systems can be applied to study biological phenomena, linking cellular-level processes such as exosome release with measurable physical outputs like muscle contraction.
